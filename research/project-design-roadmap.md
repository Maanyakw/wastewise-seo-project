# WasteWise – Project Design & SEO Implementation Roadmap

## 1. Project Overview

**Project Name:** WasteWise

**Project Type:** SEO-Optimized Waste Management and Recycling Information Portal

**Primary Niche:** Waste management, recycling, and sustainable household waste practices

**Target Market:** India

**Website:** https://wastewiseproject.tech

**Platform:** WordPress

**Hosting Environment:** Microsoft Azure Virtual Machine

**Primary Objective:**

WasteWise is an informational website designed to provide structured, practical, and search-engine-optimized information about household waste management, waste segregation, recycling, e-waste, plastic waste, and composting.

The website organizes information according to user search intent and aims to provide an easy-to-navigate content structure. Its SEO strategy combines keyword research, topic clusters, internal linking, useful content, and technical website optimization.

## 2. Problem Statement

People search online for practical information related to waste segregation, recycling, e-waste disposal, plastic waste management, composting, and household waste management.

However, relevant information may be distributed across government websites, educational resources, sustainability websites, industry sources, and individual articles. Users may need to visit several sources to understand different aspects of household waste management.

WasteWise aims to organize relevant information into a structured information portal with clear navigation, dedicated topic sections, internal linking, and search-intent-focused content. The objective is to make waste-management information easier to discover and understand, particularly for Indian households and students.

This is the project's initial problem hypothesis. Its search demand and competitive opportunities will be further evaluated through keyword metrics and SERP research.

| Guides / Blog Articles | Individual long-tail queries | Question-based and related semantic terms | Informational |
| About | WasteWise brand and project information | waste-management information portal | Navigational / informational |

### 6.1 Avoiding Keyword Cannibalization

Keyword cannibalization can occur when multiple pages target the same query with substantially overlapping content and compete for the same search intent.

WasteWise will reduce this risk by:

1. Assigning a primary query or closely related query group to each page.
2. Giving supporting articles a specific purpose and distinct search intent.
3. Linking supporting articles to their relevant topic pages.
4. Avoiding unnecessary duplication between category pages and individual guides.
5. Reviewing page titles, headings, content, and URLs before publishing new articles.
6. Updating the mapping when the actual site structure changes.

The final keyword map should use actual published WordPress URLs after the relevant pages have been created.

## 13. Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Potential Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Unverified keyword metrics | Weak or unsupported keyword priorities | Collect metrics using a keyword research tool and record the research date |
| Incomplete page coverage | Planned navigation may lead to missing destinations | Publish pages before linking them in the main navigation |
| Overlapping target keywords | Multiple pages may compete for similar queries | Maintain a keyword-to-page map and distinguish search intent |
| Inaccurate disposal guidance | Users may receive misleading environmental information | Reference reliable official and local sources |
| Technical configuration errors | Website availability or HTTPS may be affected | Verify DNS, HTTPS, Apache, and WordPress after relevant changes |
| Limited server resources | Performance or stability issues | Monitor resource usage and avoid unnecessary services |
| Missing evidence | Work may be difficult to assess | Capture screenshots and relevant command output during implementation |
